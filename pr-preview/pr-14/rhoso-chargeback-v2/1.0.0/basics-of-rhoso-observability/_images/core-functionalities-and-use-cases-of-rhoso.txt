In this module, we will explore the core functionalities and practical use cases of Red Hat OpenStack Services on OpenShift, also known as RHOSO Observability. Understanding these capabilities is essential for effectively managing, troubleshooting, and optimizing your RHOSO environment.

Let’s begin with the core functionalities. 

One of the primary strengths of RHOSO Observability is its integrated telemetry collection. This unified approach ensures there are no blind spots across your entire stack. The system collects two primary types of data. First, it gathers technical metrics, covering both your running workloads—such as CPU utilization, memory consumption, and network I/O—and OpenStack cloud data, like compute, storage, and network usage. Second, it provides comprehensive log aggregation. By collecting logs from the OpenStack control plane running on OpenShift, as well as the OpenStack data plane and services, you gain a centralized source of truth that is critical for both debugging and auditing.

Next, we move to data storage and analysis. RHOSO Observability consolidates this telemetry into a unified platform by leveraging the robust components built into OpenShift. Specifically, the Cluster Observability Operator and the Cluster Logging Operator are used to provision the tools needed to collect, store, and process your metrics and logs. A key component here is the `telemetry-operator`, which bridges the gap to ensure that OpenStack-specific data is correctly routed and processed within the OpenShift framework. For administrators, this means you have a centralized monitoring interface; the OpenShift Console UI serves as the single point for visualizing dashboards and reviewing logs, providing a familiar and consistent experience.

Finally, these tools provide enhanced troubleshooting and diagnostics. By integrating metrics and logs, administrators can perform faster root cause analysis, pinpointing the source of service outages or performance degradation, which significantly reduces your mean time to resolution. Furthermore, you can utilize real-time monitoring through intuitive dashboards to proactively identify potential issues before they impact your end-users.

Now, let's discuss the practical use cases for RHOSO Observability.

First is proactive monitoring and alerting. You can perform continuous system health checks across all OpenStack services—such as Nova, Neutron, Cinder, Glance, and Keystone—while simultaneously monitoring the underlying OpenShift infrastructure. You can also configure custom, threshold-based alerts to notify you of deviations from normal behavior, such as API service failures or network saturation.

Second, consider performance optimization and capacity planning. Observability data allows you to identify performance bottlenecks and make data-driven decisions about scaling your environment. This ensures you have adequate resources to meet future demands without the costs associated with unnecessary overprovisioning. Note that while this data helps with planning, specific resource optimization tasks are handled by other services like OpenStack Watcher, which is outside the scope of this particular training.

Third, the platform is vital for troubleshooting and debugging. When issues occur, having aggregated logs from across the entire stack drastically accelerates root cause analysis, eliminating the need to manually sift through disparate data sources.

Fourth, RHOSO Observability supports security auditing and compliance. You can track user activities and API calls to monitor for unauthorized access or policy violations. Additionally, the log data provides the necessary foundation for generating reports that demonstrate adherence to internal and regulatory compliance requirements.

As a final note on strategy, if you are transitioning from previous deployments, it is important to understand that RHOSO Observability is the designated replacement for the Service Telemetry Framework, or STF, starting in RHOSO 18. This change represents a strategic shift toward a more integrated, OpenShift-native approach, aligning your operations with modern, cloud-native observability practices.
