Welcome to this introduction to Red Hat OpenStack Services on OpenShift, commonly referred to as RHOSO. 

RHOSO represents a significant evolution in how we deploy and manage OpenStack environments. At its core, RHOSO allows OpenStack to run directly as a workload on top of the Red Hat OpenShift Container Platform. This architectural shift moves away from traditional deployment models, enabling you to manage virtual machines and bare metal resources within the modern, cloud-native framework of OpenShift.

This integration provides two major advantages. First, it offers unified management and operations. By deploying OpenStack services as a workload on OpenShift, administrators can leverage the powerful orchestration, automation, and lifecycle management features of OpenShift across the entire infrastructure stack, from containers to virtual machines. 

Second, it provides enhanced observability. Moving to this layered architecture required a complete re-architecture of how telemetry signals are collected and stored. RHOSO Observability is designed to provide comprehensive insight into your environment, with a specific focus on three areas.

First, it leverages existing OpenShift tooling. By integrating seamlessly with tools like the Cluster Observability Operator, the Cluster Logging Operator, and the OpenShift Console UI, it provides a consistent monitoring experience for those already familiar with OpenShift.

Second, it retains support for OpenStack telemetry. While embracing new tools, RHOSO maintains compatibility with native OpenStack solutions like Ceilometer and AODH, ensuring that OpenStack administrators can continue to rely on the solutions they know.

Finally, this approach improves integration with the broader OpenShift ecosystem. By building on the foundation of OpenShift, RHOSO enables more streamlined workflows and better consolidation of your operational tooling.
